Affiliate disclosure

The full statement, the placement, and the rules it is written against.

The required statement

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

This exact wording appears in the strip directly below the masthead at the top of every page on this site, and again in the footer. Amazon's Operating Agreement requires this statement, or a substantially similar one previously allowed, to be stated clearly and prominently wherever Program Content is displayed.

What the links are

Counterwise participates in the Amazon Services LLC Associates Program, an affiliate advertising program designed to provide a means for sites to earn advertising fees by advertising and linking to Amazon.com. When you click a product link on this site and buy something, Amazon may pay this site a commission. The price you pay is the same either way.

How the links are marked

Every link that leaves this site for Amazon carries three marks:

  • A visible “Sponsored” label inside the button, so the nature of the link is clear before it is clicked.
  • A rel="sponsored" value on the anchor, per search-engine link-attribute conventions.
  • A rel="nofollow" value and a new-tab target, so the click is explicit rather than accidental.

The FTC's guidance is that a disclosure should be difficult to miss and placed near the thing it qualifies, not only on a separate page a reader has to hunt for. That is why the disclosure is on every page rather than only here.

What we do not show, and why

No prices, star ratings, or review counts. Amazon's Program Policies allow a site to display price and availability only if Amazon serves the link containing that data, or if the data is obtained through the Creators API or Product Advertising API with a required date and time stamp and a disclaimer. Customer reviews and star ratings may be displayed only when obtained through those APIs. Neither condition is met on this site.

What we are not claiming

  • We are not claiming these products were tested. They were not.
  • We are not claiming a certification is valid because a listing title says so. Manufacturer certification claims are attributed to the listing and flagged where unconfirmed.
  • We are not claiming any product is the best. The pages compare trade-offs and stop there.
  • We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any of the brands named on this site.

The rules this is written against

  • Associates Program Operating Agreement — Section 5, identifying yourself as an Associate.
  • Associates Program Policies — Participation Requirements, price display, IP licence, trademark guidelines.
  • 16 CFR Part 255 — the FTC Endorsement Guides, including the definition of clear and conspicuous.
  • Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers — FTC staff guidance on placement.
  • Associates Central help — link-level disclosure must be clear and conspicuous.

The honest summary

The site earns money from these links, says so at the top of every page, marks every link, and shows no numbers it cannot source. Where it can check something against an official document it does, links the document, and dates the check. Where it cannot, it says so on the flagged page instead of filling the gap.
