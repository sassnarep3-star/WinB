The five-minute capacity worksheet

Measure your kitchen, not the listing. Five minutes with a tape measure and the bag of food you actually buy will tell you more than any quart figure.

The Counterwise desk · 20 November 2026 · Specification-led, not hands-on

1. Measure the home

Height under upper cabinets, counter depth from wall to front edge, internal sink width. Write them down before you look at any machine. An appliance that does not fit is a return, whatever its capacity.

2. Build a single layer

Put the bag of frozen chips or wings you actually buy on the counter. Build one flat layer in the footprint you expect the basket to have. Note how much of the bag that took.

3. Multiply by people

Multiply that layer by the number of people you usually cook for. That is your household volume.

4. Compare to stated capacity

Compare your household volume to the stated quarts, remembering the usable layer is well below the total. If your single layer needs more than about half the stated capacity, expect two batches.

5. Decide: split or single

Does anything you cook regularly fill a 3 qt basket on its own? If yes, a dual-basket unit will frustrate you. If you cook two foods at different temperatures at once, the split is the point.
