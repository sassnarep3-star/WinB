Design suggestions and the trend topic engine

Fifteen design suggestions that feed the next build, ten catchy working titles that wait for measurement, and the three paths to choose before any of it is implemented. Every item states what it changes, what it costs, and what must be known first.

15 design suggestions 10 working titles 3 paths to choose

Part one

The design brief for the next build

The shortest form of every suggestion, ready to feed into a design brief or a build prompt. The full argument for each is below.

Use this as the prompt

Each line is one change. Treat them as the brief for a build that should read as a polished marketing affiliate publication, not an audit and not generated text. Nothing here asserts a measured result or a product claim.

  1. Lead every page with one specific promise and one primary action, not a grid of choices.
  2. Write in a consistent first-person desk voice, signed with a name, a date and a reading time.
  3. Use real, captioned category photography; never pass generated art off as the reviewed unit.
  4. Open every product page with a “who it is for” verdict before the specifications.
  5. Organise the comparison by the job being decided, and explain any blank column in a sentence.
  6. Keep a gated list of catchy, question-shaped topic titles; publish one per cycle, chosen by measurement.
  7. Run a seasonal and occasion calendar one guide at a time, dated and retired.
  8. Answer one reader question in public each cycle, crediting the asker.
  9. Write headlines as the question a reader searches, and answer it in the first paragraph.
  10. Put the source, the method and the last-checked date on every claim-bearing surface.
  11. Give every page a social card that resolves to a real, correctly sized image.
  12. Cluster content by category with hub pages and cross-links that a reader would actually click.
  13. Offer only a follow path that works today; never a form that collects an address it cannot deliver.
  14. Label outbound actions with the reason for the click and keep the disclosure beside them.
  15. Keep one reader navigation group and move every oversight surface to the footer.

Part three

Read the case for each design suggestion

The board is a summary. This is the argument: why it makes the site read as published, what it costs, how it fails, and what has to be known before choosing it.

DES-01

One promise per page, one primary action

Structure For review
RecommendationEach page leads with a single specific promise and a single primary action. Not a grid of equal options, not a dashboard of metrics.
Why it reads as published
A shop page that opens with five equal choices asks the reader to do the selling. One promise and one button is what a reader acts on, and what a search result snippet can carry.
Effort
Template work, already partly done
Payoff
Immediate, on every page
If it goes wrong
Over-simplifying a comparison page. Keep the detail one scroll down.
What we need to know
A decision on how the comparison page keeps its depth without leading with it.
DES-02

A declared editorial voice and a named byline

Voice For review
RecommendationWrite in a consistent first-person desk voice, sign every page with a name, a date and a reading time, and give that name a two-line biography.
Why it reads as published
An unauthored affiliate page is the example Google gives of content likely to attract action under its site-reputation policy. A byline is the cheapest credibility signal available and it is what separates a publication from generated text.
Effort
One owner decision, then template work
Payoff
Trust and policy posture
If it goes wrong
A name without accountability is decoration. The biography must state the non-testing position plainly.
What we need to know
Q-05 — whether the owner wants to publish a real byline and take editorial responsibility.
DES-03

Photographic category art, honestly captioned

Visual For review
RecommendationUse real photographs of the category, always captioned as representative rather than as the reviewed unit. Ask the brands for licensed imagery before scraping anything.
Why it reads as published
Generic illustration is the fastest way to read as generated. Photography of a kitchen, a counter, a basket of food makes the page feel like a shop. The IP licence forbids storing listing images, so the images must be licensed or made.
Effort
One email per brand, plus generation work
Payoff
The single largest lift in perceived quality
If it goes wrong
Imagery that implies a test it did not do. Caption every image as editorial, never as the reviewed unit.
What we need to know
Q-14 — whether any brand will license its own photography.
DES-04

A “who it is for” verdict above the specifications

Structure Built
RecommendationOpen every product page with a short verdict panel: buy it if, skip it if, and also consider. Put the specifications underneath.
Why it reads as published
A buyer arrives with a job in mind. Answering “is this for me” before listing wattages is the difference between a shop and a spec sheet, and it is the part a reader acts on.
Effort
Template work, implemented
Payoff
Immediate
If it goes wrong
A verdict must not become a hands-on claim. Keep it a specification and fit judgement, labelled as such.
What we need to know
None. Maintain the rule that verdicts are specification judgements, not test results.
DES-05

Turn the comparison table into a decision table

Structure Built
RecommendationGroup the comparison by the job a reader is deciding, not by feature. Lead with format and capacity, keep the fields that are stable, and leave the rest blank with a reason.
Why it reads as published
A feature-by-feature grid is a data sheet. A table organised around a decision is a buying guide, and a table that explains why a column is blank builds more trust than a stale number.
Effort
Template work, implemented
Payoff
Immediate
If it goes wrong
Fewer columns can look thinner. The blanks must carry a sentence, not just an empty cell.
What we need to know
DQ-Q8 — external dimensions, the one decision-relevant blank still missing.
DES-06

A trend-led topic engine, gated on measurement

Content For review
RecommendationMaintain a list of catchy, question-shaped working titles tied to the two product clusters. Publish one only after the measurement baseline has run and the owner has chosen it.
Why it reads as published
Catchy titles are how a new site gets found at all: they match the question a person actually types. But publishing a burst of them without measurement is the scaled-content pattern search engines name as abuse, so each one waits for the gate.
Effort
Ongoing, one title per fortnight
Payoff
The main organic-traffic lever
If it goes wrong
A title that promises a comparison the page cannot deliver. Every published title must be answerable from sourced data.
What we need to know
DQ-02 — the 30-day baseline, and the owner picking one title at a time.
DES-07

A seasonal and occasion calendar, one guide at a time

Content For review
RecommendationKeep a short list of occasions tied to the products and publish at most one seasonal guide per cycle, dated, and retired when the season passes.
Why it reads as published
A seasonal frame matches the meal a reader is cooking tonight, which is timely without chasing a popularity claim. Publishing the whole calendar at once is the opposite of a publication.
Effort
Ongoing
Payoff
Timely without being thin
If it goes wrong
Seasonal copy goes stale. Date everything and retire it deliberately.
What we need to know
Q-11 — which occasion to cover first, and where the site is read.
DES-08

A reader question → answer loop, answered in public

Content Blocked
RecommendationCapture the best reader question each week and answer it as a short post, crediting the asker.
Why it reads as published
Reader questions are free and uncompetable topic research, and answering in public proves a human is here. It also converts a visitor into a reason to return.
Effort
Low, ongoing
Payoff
Compounding
If it goes wrong
Needs a real contact route. A form that discards input is worse than no form.
What we need to know
Q-07 — a published contact route for corrections and questions.
DES-09

Question-shaped titles that match real intent

Discovery For review
RecommendationWrite every headline as the question a person would search, then answer it in the first paragraph.
Why it reads as published
A question headline is both more clickable and more likely to match a real query than a product-name headline. It also keeps the page honest about what it answers.
Effort
Editorial discipline
Payoff
Direct
If it goes wrong
Questions that over-promise. The page must actually answer the question it poses.
What we need to know
DQ-Q6 — Search Console, to confirm which questions are actually asked.
DES-10

Credibility signals on every surface

Trust Built
RecommendationShow the source, the method, the last-checked date and the disclosure on every page that makes a claim. Never let a claim appear without the page it came from.
Why it reads as published
This is the machinery of trust: a reader can check the claim, and a crawler can see the sourcing. It is also the difference between an affiliate site and a content farm.
Effort
Implemented; ongoing discipline
Payoff
Compounding
If it goes wrong
Signalling without substance. The sources must be real and re-checked, not decorative.
What we need to know
A standing re-verification cadence, and the FLG items resolved.
DES-11

Social cards that resolve to a real image

Visual Built
RecommendationEvery page carries an og:image and twitter:image that points at a real, correctly sized file.
Why it reads as published
A social card that points at a missing file renders blank on every share. It is invisible in a link check and invisible in the build until someone looks at the share dialog.
Effort
Implemented; guarded in the build
Payoff
Immediate
If it goes wrong
Images that are too small or mislabelled. The build now fails if the file is missing.
What we need to know
None. The gate re-checks on every build.
DES-12

Topic clusters and a hub page per category

Discovery Built
RecommendationGive each category a hub, link every guide in that category to it, and link the hubs to each other and to the comparison.
Why it reads as published
Clusters tell a search engine what the site is about and tell a reader where to go next. They are the cheapest authority a new site can build, and they keep the site coherent as it grows.
Effort
Implemented; ongoing discipline
Payoff
Compounding
If it goes wrong
Over-linking reads as SEO. Link where a reader would actually click.
What we need to know
None beyond the topic engine that adds to the clusters.
DES-13

A working follow path, never a dead form

Conversion For review
RecommendationOffer the RSS feed now, because it works today. Add an email form only once a provider, a consent notice and a delivery path all exist.
Why it reads as published
A capture form that collects an address it cannot deliver to is worse than no form: it promises a relationship the site cannot honour. RSS is a real path that costs nothing to offer.
Effort
Half a day once a provider is chosen
Payoff
Audience building
If it goes wrong
Collecting addresses without a lawful basis. The notice must precede the first address.
What we need to know
Q-04 — provider, consent language, and lawful basis.
DES-14

“Why this link” microcopy on outbound buttons

Conversion For review
RecommendationLabel each outbound action with the reason for it, and repeat the affiliate disclosure beside it.
Why it reads as published
A bare “Buy on Amazon” is fine, but “See it on Amazon — affiliate link” tells the reader exactly what the click is. Clarity converts better than a button that hides its purpose, and it is the honest form.
Effort
Small, per page
Payoff
Trust and clarity
If it goes wrong
Clutter. Keep the microcopy to one line and keep the disclosure where it belongs.
What we need to know
A decision on wording, and confirmation that disclosure stays above the fold.
DES-15

Keep the oversight machinery out of the reader’s way

Structure Built
RecommendationOne reader navigation group. Every audit, register, flag and dashboard surface lives in the footer, reachable but not leading.
Why it reads as published
A shop that opens with its own audit trail reads as a dashboard, not a shop. The oversight surfaces are necessary; they should not be the front page.
Effort
Implemented
Payoff
Immediate
If it goes wrong
Oversight surfaces must still be reachable. The footer is the single index of them.
What we need to know
None.

Part six

Choose a path before anything is built

These three paths are the decision the owner makes before any design suggestion or topic is implemented. They differ in what gets built first, not in the compliance rules, which are fixed.

A · The safest shop. Products first, guides second, measurement last.

Path A — Compliance-first shop

Close FLG-001 (tagging), keep the no-price rule, publish on the fortnightly cadence, and defer any further seasonal guides until measured (the 2026-11-20 winter batch is the recorded exception).

Cost: Slowest growth. The site stays a specification shop until a review unit is bought (SUG-01).
Best if: The owner wants zero policy risk and is happy to grow slowly.
B · The most readable site. Byline, clusters, seasonal notebook and worksheets first.

Path B — Publication-first authority

Name the byline (Q-05), publish the capacity worksheet (SUG-04), run the seasonal notebook one guide at a time (SUG-09), keep the gate on every build.

Cost: More writing before more revenue. Still needs SUG-01 for hands-on content.
Best if: The owner wants a site that reads as a publication and earns links.
C · The most data-driven site. Endpoint, Search Console and ledger first.

Path C — Measurement-first growth

Deploy the collector (SUG-03), submit the sitemap (SUG-13), choose every next topic from measured queries, keep the ledger empty until a first-party report exists.

Cost: Nothing to read until the endpoint is deployed. The shop stays as it is for 30 days.
Best if: The owner wants every future decision to be a data question.

How to use this page

Start at Part one if you want the shortest version, or Part two if you want the decisions. A suggestion becomes real when somebody reads it, decides, and changes the build — not because it appears here. The ones already in the site are labelled “Built”.

For the topics in Part four, the discipline matters more than the list. Pick one title, satisfy its gate, publish it, and let the measurement baseline decide the next. The whole engine is a hypothesis that has to earn its place.

What this page deliberately does not do

It does not score the suggestions or predict an outcome. No measurement has run, so any figure would be invented. It also does not claim that any title is popular, that any product is better, or that any link is compliant until its tag status is confirmed.

Design and topic suggestions are editorial opinions. The compliance rules they sit under — disclosure, tagged and qualified links, no prices or ratings, no hands-on claims — are not opinions and are not on the table.
