Product photos rarely settle the practical questions of ventilation, storage, or routine cleanup. The exact model's care instructions and dimensions do. Accessory materials and dishwasher guidance can vary even within one product family, so read the manual for the model number you're buying — not the family page.

Questions worth answering before it has a permanent spot

  • Which parts touch food every session? Basket, tray, crisper plate, paddle. Count them; that number is your nightly dish pile. The listing's "What's included" and the manual's cleaning section are the two sources that agree.
  • Does the maker's language say dishwasher-safe — for which parts, and with what caveats? "Nonstick, dishwasher safe" in a title can still carry a hand-wash-only note for the coating's longevity in the manual. Don't assume a removable part is dishwasher-safe unless the maker confirms it for that model.
  • Where do hot components cool, and what must they not touch? A counter needs a trivet zone; a narrow galley kitchen might not have one that's out of the traffic lane.
  • What clearance does the manual require around the vents? Many listings omit this; the manual never should. Wall gaps, cabinet overhangs, and cord length decide whether the unit can stay out — where most "too much fuss" returns really come from.
  • Can it live on the counter full-time in your house? If the honest answer is no, compare storage height and weight too: a 26-qt oven-style unit that needs both hands and a shelf is a different ownership experience than a basket that nests.

A cheap test that predicts 80% of regret

Before ordering, do one imaginary dinner start to finish: open the box, place the unit with vent clearance, load, run, dump crumbs, wash the counted parts, dry, store. Every "ugh" in that rehearsal is a listing question to ask now rather than a return note later.
