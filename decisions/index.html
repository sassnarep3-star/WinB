Choose first

Three paths — pick one before anything is implemented

Each path is a coherent bet. Mixing them is how a site ends up with seasonal guides, no measurement and no byline. The desk recommends Path B first, Path C in parallel: the publication polish is already built, and the measurement endpoint is the only thing standing between instinct and data.

Three coherent paths. Choose one; the suggestions below are ordered to serve it.
PathPromiseFirst stepsCostBest if
Path A — Compliance-first shopThe safest shop. Products first, guides second, measurement last.Close FLG-001 (tagging), keep the no-price rule, publish on the fortnightly cadence, defer seasonal guides until measured.Slowest growth. The site stays a specification shop until a review unit is bought (SUG-01).The owner wants zero policy risk and is happy to grow slowly.
Path B — Publication-first authorityThe most readable site. Byline, clusters, seasonal notebook and worksheets first.Name the byline (Q-05), publish the capacity worksheet (SUG-04), run the seasonal notebook one guide at a time (SUG-09), keep the gate on every build.More writing before more revenue. Paths are editorial recommendations, not factual claims. The policy constraints they respect are cited in the suggestions below: disclosure A1 §5, tag and price rules A2 §2(b), no untried-experience claims F1, sponsored marking G1, cadence and authorship G2.

How to choose

Answer three questions in order: (1) Is the tagging question closed (SUG-02)? If not, stop and close it — no path earns anything until then. (2) Does the owner want a named byline (Q-05)? If yes, Path B. (3) Is the analytics endpoint deployed (SUG-03)? If not, Path C is the next task regardless of the path chosen. Record the choice here with a date; the next session reads it before writing anything.

Part one

Suggestions, in the order they should be reviewed

Priority 1 means “do this before writing another word”. Every item states its risk, because a recommendation without a failure mode is marketing.

P1

Buy and document one review unit

Not started
RecommendationStart with the cheapest of the five. Buy it, photograph the unboxing, publish the receipt.
Why it matters
Every page currently says “we have not handled this”. One disclosed bought-and-tested unit converts the whole site from specification summary to review, which is the only durable defence against both Google’s thin-affiliation problem and reader distrust.
Effort
Cost of one unit, 2–3 days
Payoff
Highest of anything on this page
Risk
A single unit does not generalise. Do not let it become “we tested everything”.
P1

Close the tagging question before writing anything else

Needs owner access
RecommendationOpen Associates Central, mint one link with the tag, resolve it, and compare with the supplied short links.
Why it matters
If the supplied links do not credit the account, every article published between now and then earns nothing. This is the cheapest possible check with the largest possible downside.
Effort
Under an hour
Payoff
Protects all future revenue
Risk
None.
P2

Deploy the analytics collector and let it run for 30 days

Source shipped, not deployed
RecommendationDeploy the supplied worker, set one endpoint, and treat the first 30 days as a baseline rather than a scoreboard.
Why it matters
Without measurement the next topic is chosen by instinct — the exact failure mode this project set out to avoid. Thirty days of page_view plus outbound_click is enough to rank the existing guides honestly.
Effort
Half a day, then nothing
Payoff
Turns topic choice into a data question
Risk
Small samples mislead. Do not compute a conversion rate from 12 clicks.
P2

Write the capacity guide as the flagship, with real measuring instructions

Draft published, needs a measurement worksheet
RecommendationGive readers a method for measuring their own basket, shelf and counter, rather than a table of quarts they can get from the listing.
Why it matters
It is the one topic where this desk can genuinely be more useful than a product page: arithmetic and a tape measure. Quarts are a marketing unit; food is a physical object.
Effort
1–2 days
Payoff
Earns links; a real answer to a real query
Risk
The tone must stay practical. A framework with no numbers of its own is still thin.
P3

Publish on a fixed cadence and stop

Proposed
RecommendationOne substantial guide every two weeks, announced in the feed, added to the sitemap automatically. Not daily, not whenever inspiration arrives.
Why it matters
Google treats a burst of near-identical pages as scaled content abuse. A slow, steady, original cadence reads as a publication; a 40-page month reads as a content farm. The build already regenerates the sitemap and feed, so cadence costs nothing but writing.
Effort
Ongoing
Payoff
Compounding
Risk
Miss two cycles and the site looks abandoned. Two weeks is achievable; daily is not.
P3

Answer one real question per week from the contact inbox, in public

Blocked on Q-07
RecommendationTurn the best reader question each week into a short post, credited to the asker.
Why it matters
Reader questions are free, uncompetable keyword research. They also prove a human is here.
Effort
Low
Payoff
Medium, compounding
Risk
Requires a real contact route (Q-07) and a reply discipline.
P4

Do not add categories until one category converts

Standing recommendation
RecommendationResist expanding to coffee, sous vide, blenders. Five products in two closely-related categories is a coherent shop; twelve scattered products is a directory.
Why it matters
Topical coherence is the cheapest authority a new site can accumulate. Every new category halves the depth available to the existing ones.
Effort
Restraint
Payoff
Protects the whole site
Risk
Feels slow. It is slow.
P2

Run the seasonal notebook as hypotheses, not as a content burst

Framing implemented; guides not yet published
RecommendationFrame the four cooking moments on the front page as editorial hypotheses. Publish at most one seasonal guide per fortnight, only after 30 days of measurement (SUG-03).
Why it matters
Seasonal framing matches the meal a reader is cooking tonight, which is the cheapest way to feel current without chasing popularity claims. Publishing four seasonal guides at once would be the scaled-content pattern Google names as abuse.
Effort
One guide per fortnight
Payoff
Timely without being thin
Risk
Seasonal copy goes stale. Date every seasonal guide and retire it when the season passes.
P3

Publish a named byline and a dated byline block

Template implemented; name pending Q-05
RecommendationAdd the author, the date and the reading time to every guide and product page, and a two-line biography on the About page. Owner to supply the name (Q-05).
Why it matters
Google’s site-reputation policy weighs stated authorship, and an unauthored affiliate page is its own example of content likely to attract action. A byline costs nothing and changes the policy posture.
Effort
One owner decision, then template work (done)
Payoff
Trust and policy posture
Risk
A byline without accountability is decoration. The biography must state the non-testing position plainly.
P4

Keep generated illustrations until licensed photography exists

Standing rule
RecommendationDo not scrape or cache Amazon listing images. Ask the two brands for licensed photography (Q-14); until then, keep the generated illustrations and label them as editorial art.
Why it matters
The IP License forbids storing or caching Product Advertising Content images. A static site is a cache, so listing photography cannot be used here even if it looks better.
Effort
One email per brand
Payoff
Better imagery without a licence breach
Risk
Generated art can look generic. Caption it honestly rather than passing it off as a photograph.

Files shipped; submission pending owner
RecommendationSubmit sitemap.xml in Search Console, subscribe to the feed, and after 30 days choose the next topic from measured queries instead of instinct.
Why it matters
The build already generates both files from the same data as the pages, so a new guide cannot ship without being listed. Submission is the step that turns files into indexing.
Effort
Under an hour, then waiting
Payoff
The next topic is a data question
Risk
Search Console needs a verification token only the owner can supply (FLG-008).
P4

Gate every AI step behind the compliance check

Standing rule
RecommendationAutomate research, drafting and scheduling, but never automate the compliance gate. The R1–R10 check runs on every build and fails the publish on any breach.
Why it matters
Automation is what makes this site scalable; the gate is what makes it trustworthy. Removing the gate to move faster would publish the exact failures the gate exists to catch.
Effort
Standing rule
Payoff
Scalable without being reckless
Risk
A gate that is skipped once is a gate that no longer exists. CI enforces it.

P3

Add a comparison table that refuses to guess on price

Implemented
RecommendationCompare on the things that are stable and knowable: baskets, format, window, stated capacity, app dependency. Send the reader to Amazon for price.
Why it matters
Price changes hourly and cannot legally be printed here. A table that explains *why* it has no price column builds more trust than one that shows a stale number.
Effort
Done in this build
Payoff
Immediate
Risk
None, as long as the no-price rule is not quietly broken later.
P3

Tighten the two clusters with cross-links

Implemented
RecommendationLink every air-fryer guide to the air-fryer hub and every thermometer guide to the thermometer hub, and link the two hubs to each other and to the comparison table.
Why it matters
Clusters tell search engines what the site is about and tell readers where to go next. The build now does this on every product and guide page; the remaining work is to keep it true as guides are added.
Effort
Done in this build; ongoing discipline
Payoff
Compounding internal authority
Risk
Over-linking reads as SEO. Link where a reader would actually click.

Q-01 Blocked Did the five supplied short links come out of the shopnow047c-20 Associates account?
It decides whether referrals are credited at all. The observed targets carry no tag parameter.		 All five resolve to live listings. None of the resolved URLs carries tag=shopnow047c-20. Whether Amazon applies the account tag at click time for links minted inside the dashboard. Generate one short link inside Associates Central while signed in, resolve it, and compare its parameters with the supplied set. Five minutes in the dashboard settles it.
Q-02 Open Which channel is expected to produce the first 100 sessions?
A new project site with no domain authority will not rank for competitive head terms in its first month. The first-traction plan should name a realistic channel rather than assuming search.		 No analytics are live, so current traffic is unmeasured (FLG-007). Any existing audience the owner already has: a list, a social following, a community. Owner inventories channels they can reach today. If the answer is “none”, the plan should be paid-or-pinned distribution and long-tail queries, not a head-term bet.
Q-03 Open Is a review unit obtainable for any of the five products?
Hands-on content is the single biggest differentiator available, and the current pages are legally barred from pretending to it (FLG-004).		 No unit is held. The listing data is thorough enough for specification content only. Whether any brand will supply a unit to a project with no traffic history. Ask the two brands directly. If the answer is no, buy the cheapest unit and document the purchase — a disclosed bought-and-tested unit is still a test.
Q-04 Open Which mailing provider will receive captured addresses?
The capture form currently collects nothing usable (FLG-008). A form that silently discards input is worse than no form.		 The form fires a capture_intent event and does not transmit an address. Provider, consent language, and the lawful basis for storage. Pick a provider with a documented EU/US data story, then wire ENDPOINT and consent copy in one change.
Q-05 Open Who owns the site — a person or a desk?
Google’s site-reputation policy explicitly weighs “stated or implied authorship”, and an unauthored affiliate page with no commercial disclaimer is the example it gives of content likely to attract action.		 Pages currently sign as “The Counterwise desk” with a stated non-testing policy. Whether the owner wants to publish a real byline and take editorial responsibility publicly. Owner decision. A named author with a two-line biography is cheap and materially improves both trust and policy posture.
Q-06 Open Will the site move to a custom domain?
A github.io subpath is a fine host but a weak brand home; it also makes Search Console property setup and cookie scope awkward.		 Current origin is a GitHub Pages project path. Whether a domain will be bought, and who controls DNS. Owner decision, then a CNAME file and a Search Console domain property.
Q-07 Open What is the contact route for corrections?
A publication without a contact route cannot be corrected, and reviewers notice.		 No email address is published. The repository issue tracker is the only route. Whether the owner wants a public address. Create one and publish it, or formalise the issue tracker as the route.
Q-08 Answered Can prices and star ratings be displayed on a static affiliate page?
It determines whether comparison tables can carry prices at all.		 No — not without Amazon serving the link or Creators API / PA API data. The site therefore shows no prices or ratings. — Answered from Participation Requirements §2(b).
Q-09 Answered What disclosure wording must appear?
It is a material-breach risk, not a courtesy.		 Operating Agreement §5 specifies “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” The site carries it under the masthead on every page and again in the footer. — Answered from the Operating Agreement.
Q-10 Answered How should outbound product links be marked for search engines?
Unqualified paid links breach Google’s link-spam policy; the fix is a one-attribute change.		 rel="sponsored" for advertisements and paid placements. This site uses rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" on every Amazon link. — Answered from Google Search Central.
Q-11 Open Which seasonal moments should the notebook cover first?
Seasonal framing helps a reader find the guide that matches the meal in front of them, but picking the wrong season first wastes the cadence.		 Four cooking moments are drafted as editorial hypotheses (weeknight two-food dinners, big-batch sharing, holiday roasts and leftovers, summer grilling). None is a claim about search volume or popularity. Which moment the owner’s audience actually cooks, and in which hemisphere the site is read. Owner picks one moment for the next guide, or waits for 30 days of page_view data (SUG-03) and lets measured queries choose. Do not publish all four at once.
Q-12 Open What may the AI pipeline do, and what must a human approve?
The brief asks for an AI-powered platform that automates research, content and optimisation. Automation without a gate is how a site publishes an unverified price or a hands-on claim it cannot support.		 The build already automates sitemap, feed, link checks and the R1–R10 compliance gate. No AI step writes product facts; every spec comes from the listing record. Whether the owner wants AI drafting of guide prose, and under what review rule. Owner approves the automation table in the Data Room: automated steps stay automated, editorial and compliance steps stay human-gated. Any AI draft is treated as a suggestion until a human checks it against a source.
Q-13 Open Stay on github.io or move to a custom domain?
A project path is a fine host but a weak brand home, and Search Console domain properties are awkward on a subpath.		 Current origin is https://sassnarep3-star.github.io/WinB/ (Pages legacy build, main branch, path /). Domain budget, DNS control, and whether brand matters enough to pay for it. Owner decision. If yes, add a CNAME file, set up a domain property in Search Console, and keep the github.io URLs as redirects.
Q-14 Open Which product images may be shown?
Readers expect photography, but Amazon’s IP License forbids storing or caching Product Advertising Content images. A static site is a cache.		 The site uses generated editorial illustrations and states so. No Amazon product photography is stored. Whether any brand will license its own photography for this site. Ask the two brands for licensed images. Until then, keep generated illustrations and label them. Do not scrape listing images.

The pattern in the open questions
