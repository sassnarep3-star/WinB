Part two

The prompt these directions feed

This is the block to hand to a designer, or to a build agent. It carries the goals, the house style, the anti-slop rules and the hard constraints in one piece. Edit it freely — it is a starting point, and the site it describes is already partly built.

Copy this. Everything the site has to be, in one prompt.
The promptWhy it is worded this way
 
You are designing and building a small, honest Amazon Associates
publication for five kitchen products: three air fryers and two meat
thermometers. It is a shop that reads like a magazine, not a link directory.

WHAT THIS SITE IS
- Five products, each chosen for a different job. No product beats another.
- Every factual claim comes from the manufacturer's own Amazon listing, is
  labelled as the manufacturer's claim, and carries a link to that listing.
- No price, no star rating and no Amazon popularity position is shown anywhere: the policy only
  permits a price when Amazon serves the link carrying it, or when the data
  comes from the Creators API under its licence, and this is a static site.
- Nothing is described as "we tested it" unless a unit was actually bought and
  handled. If nothing was handled, say so in plain words.
- Where a value cannot be confirmed from an official source, leave the cell
  empty and label it unverified. Never infer, average or estimate.

THE READER
Someone with a specific kitchen job who is choosing between three shapes of
machine and does not trust a list. They arrive from a search with a question.
Answer it in the first three sentences.

VOICE AND LAYOUT
- Warm, plain, specific. Confident without adjectives. No superlatives.
- Serif display type for headings, a neutral sans for text, one warm accent
  colour, generous white space, real margins.
- Editorial photography, captioned as representative of the category and
  never as the reviewed unit.
- One navigation group. Everything else lives in the footer.
- The required affiliate disclosure sits directly under the masthead on every
  page and is repeated in the footer, and every outbound product link is
  marked rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" and carries the Associate tag.

WHAT MAKES A PAGE
1. The decision first: buy it if / skip it if / also consider.
2. Then the evidence: the specification grid, every cell with its source.
3. Then the limits: what this page cannot tell you, and why.
4. Then the next click: the comparison table or the category hub.

ANTI-SLOP RULES
- No "Top 10", no "Ultimate Guide", no "Game-Changing", no "Hidden Gem".
- No page that exists only because a keyword existed.
- No AI-generated review language. No listicle of forty thin sections.
- No uncited statistics, and no "studies show".
- Publish on a fixed cadence chosen from measured queries, not from instinct.
- Every table row carries a source link. Every claim can be checked.

WHAT TO BUILD FIRST
A front page that leads with the five picks, two category hubs, five product
pages, five question-shaped guides, one comparison table, and a reviewable
suggestions room that a person can use to choose what happens next.
 Every constraint in it is enforced by the build: the disclosure placement by R1, the sponsored marking by R2, the absence of prices and ratings by R3, the absence of hands-on claims by R4, and the source on every table row by R15. A prompt that cannot be checked is a wish, and this one can. The rules themselves are written out in How we choose, and what is still unresolved is in Flagged for Review.

Part three

How this site is meant to earn a search visitor

Organic traffic comes from being the page that answers the question, not from tricks. Each lever below is something the build already does, with the official rule that justifies it. A lever with no rule behind it would be an opinion, and it is not on this list.

The one lever that is still closed

Measuring which lever works. Until the collector endpoint is deployed (DQ-02), the site cannot tell which of these is earning anything, and the next guide is chosen by judgement rather than by evidence. That is the honest state of it, and it is a decision for the owner rather than a defect in the build.

How to read the “What is known” column

Known means it is recorded somewhere official or somewhere in this site’s own register, with a link. It never means it is generally accepted, and it never means the desk has tested anything.

Part five

The tells of an AI-generated affiliate site, and what this one does instead

The brief says the site should not read like slop. “Slop” is not a matter of taste; it is a short list of specific habits, each of which breaks a rule that somebody published. Here they are, with the rule.

Six tells. Each one names the official rule it breaks, so the line is not drawn by preference.
The tellWhy it is not done hereSource
A top-ten list of five productsThere are five products and they do different jobs. A ranking would be a ranking of jobs.the rule
Prices and star ratings copied from the listingThe policy permits a price only when Amazon serves it; a rating is Amazon’s content, not this site’s endorsement.the rule
“We tested it” on a product nobody handledFTC guidance is explicit: you cannot describe your experience with a product you have not tried.the rule
A wall of thin sections, one per keywordBulk near-identical generation is the definition of scaled content abuse.the rule
An email form that quietly goes nowhereIt collects nothing, so it promises something the site cannot deliver. Removed; the RSS feed is the follow path.the rule
A traffic or revenue number with no report behind itNothing has been measured, so any such number would be invented.the rule
What is here now

Five products, five guides, two clusters

5 product pages, 5 guides, a comparison table, and a reviewable suggestions room. Every one of them reachable from the front page in two clicks.

What is not here yet

A reader, a click, and a reviewed unit

No traffic has been measured, no link has been confirmed to credit the account, and nothing has been handled. The three decisions above are what change that.
