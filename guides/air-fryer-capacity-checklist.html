A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts, litres, baskets, and the difference between "6 quart" meaning one drawer or two. A checklist you can run against any listing in two minutes.

By The Crisp Kitchen Editorial Desk · 2026-11-20 · 5 min read Topics are working hypotheses, not keyword data

Capacity numbers are the most compared and least useful spec in the category, because a quart means something different in each shape. Here is a two-minute checklist that survives contact with a real listing.

The checklist

  1. Find out how many compartments the number covers. "6 quart" can be one drawer or two 3-quart drawers. The listing title usually says "dual basket" or "2 independent 3QT" — the Chefman dual basket says both.
  2. Ask whether the number includes the crisper plate. Some capacities are measured to the rim with the insert removed. If the listing does not say, treat the number as a maximum, not a working volume.
  3. Compare the shape, not the volume. Eight quarts in a shallow wide drawer cooks differently from eight quarts in a tall narrow one. Look at the interior dimensions if the listing gives them; if it does not, that is missing information worth noting.
  4. Convert to portions you recognise. A useful anchor: a 3-quart basket is roughly a single batch of fries for two people. If your household is four, one 3-quart side is one round, not a meal.
  5. Check the height against your counter. A big oven-style unit needs headroom under cabinets. Measure the gap, then look for the exterior height — not the capacity.
  6. Write down what is missing. If the listing does not state interior dimensions, exterior dimensions, or watts, that is not a reason to guess. It is a reason to ask the seller.

What this site deliberately does not do

It does not convert quarts into "serves X people". That conversion depends on what you cook, and a confident-looking number would be invented. The portion anchor above is a rough rule of thumb, labelled as such, not a manufacturer specification.
