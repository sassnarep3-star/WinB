Your first air fryer: five questions that decide it before you spend anything

You do not need a feature comparison to choose well. You need five answers about your own kitchen.

Published 2026-11-20 · 3 minute read · Question-led guide, not a product test

A first air fryer is a simple purchase that people overthink. Almost every regret in this category comes from answering a feature question instead of a kitchen question.

One: how many people are you usually cooking for?

Not how many you are having a party for. The number you are feeding on a Tuesday. Write it down. It is the single biggest determinant of whether you need a compact single basket, a family-size basket, or two smaller ones.

Two: what is the widest single thing you cook?

A whole chicken, a large fish, a tray of vegetables, a frozen pizza. Measure it. The usable width of the chamber is a hard limit that no feature list will tell you about, and the number is usually smaller than the total capacity figure suggests.

Three: how much counter and how much headroom?

Width, depth, and the clearance above the unit. Air fryers move a lot of hot air and need somewhere for it to go. If you are putting one under a cabinet, the clearance requirement in the manual is the number that decides it.

Four: how many parts come apart?

Basket, tray, divider, handle, seal. Every removable part is a thing to wash and a thing to store. Check which ones the manufacturer says are dishwasher-safe, because that turns the recurring chore from a decision into a non-decision.

Five: what will you cook on weeknights, specifically?

Not what you could cook. What you will. Frozen fries, a chicken breast, reheated leftovers, roasted vegetables. If your list fits in a single basket, buy a single basket and keep the money. If your list has two components that need different treatment at the same time, a dual basket earns its place.
