A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts are a volume. Dinner is an area problem. Here is the twenty-minute check that stops a wrong purchase.

By Counterwise editorial Updated 2026-11-20 Reading time 6 min Basis published specifications and public guidance

In this guide

  2. The four measurements that decide it
  3. Match the basket to your largest regular meal

Why the quart number misleads

A manufacturer measures capacity the way a suitcase maker measures luggage: generously, and in the most favourable dimension. Two air fryers both sold as 6 quarts can differ by a third in the flat area you can actually load.

The number also says nothing about crowding. Air frying works by moving hot air over the surface of food. Crowd the basket and you are steaming, not frying. A smaller basket used properly beats a larger one packed full.

The four measurements that decide it

Interior floor width and depth, measured inside the basket or on the rack. Interior height above the floor, which decides whether a chicken or a tall roast fits. Rack positions, if the model has any. Exterior depth plus door or drawer clearance, which decides whether it works on your counter.

Write these four numbers down for any candidate machine. If a listing does not state them, that is itself information.

Match the basket to your largest regular meal

Count what you cook most weeks, not what you cook at Thanksgiving. If your regular meal is a protein for two plus a vegetable, you need roughly enough flat area for both without stacking. If it is a whole chicken, you need height.

A useful rule of thumb: one layer of food with visible gaps between pieces. Anything tighter and the machine behaves like an oven with the door ajar.

Batch cooking and the second round

If you cook in batches, the relevant spec is recovery time: how fast the machine returns to temperature after the drawer opens. That number is almost never published, which is why the honest answer is that bigger is not faster.

Two drawers are a batching strategy in themselves. They let the second batch start while the first rests.

What to ignore

Preset counts. 'Family size' as an adjective. Any capacity figure expressed only in litres or quarts without interior dimensions. Photos of a full basket, which are styled for the camera and usually show an under-filled basket precisely because that is what works.

The short version

Buy the flat area you cook on most nights, with enough height for the one thing you roast whole. Everything else is decoration.
