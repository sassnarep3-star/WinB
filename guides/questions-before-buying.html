Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

A multi-function machine is a promise that one box replaces three. Here is how to check whether that promise holds for your kitchen.

By Counterwise editorial Updated 2026-11-20 Reading time 8 min Basis published specifications and public guidance

In this guide

  1. Does it replace something, or add to it?
  2. Which function is the primary one?
  3. What does the interface cost you?
  4. Where will it live?
  5. What does the warranty cover, and for how long?
  6. Would you buy it again at full price?

Does it replace something, or add to it?

The value of a multi-function machine is the counter space and the money it saves by replacing appliances you already own. If it replaces nothing, it is a new appliance with a new footprint and a new cleaning routine.

Name the appliance it replaces before you buy. If you cannot, you are buying a capability, not a replacement, and you should judge it as one.

Which function is the primary one?

Every multi-function machine is excellent at one thing and acceptable at the rest. A French-door air fryer oven that is superb at air frying may be an average toaster. A toaster oven that toasts beautifully may air-fry unevenly.

Find the function you will use most days and judge the machine on that alone. Then check that the secondary functions are good enough not to be irritating.

What does the interface cost you?

Preset counts are a proxy for how much the manufacturer expects you to use the manual controls. A machine with twenty-four presets and one dial is harder to use than one with a temperature dial and a timer, unless you memorise the card.

Look for a manual mode that is reachable without a menu, and a timer you can adjust while it runs.

Where will it live?

Counter depth, door swing, drawer pull, vent direction, and the height under your cabinets. A machine that does not fit is a machine that lives in a cupboard and gets used monthly.

Write the five numbers down and measure your actual counter before you order, not after.

What does the warranty cover, and for how long?

Check the warranty period and whether it covers the heating element and the non-stick coating separately. Coatings are the most common wear item and the least commonly covered.

Also check whether the accessories are replaceable on their own. A machine with a proprietary basket you cannot re-buy has a fixed lifespan.

Would you buy it again at full price?

The final question is the useful one. A discount is a reason to move a purchase forward, never a reason to make one. If the answer is no at list price, the answer is no.

The short version

Six questions, one of which usually settles it. None of them require reading a review score.
