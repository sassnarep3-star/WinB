How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The two costs that arrive after the purchase. What to measure on your own counter, and what each shape demands of a sink.

By The Crisp Kitchen Editorial Desk · 2026-11-20 · 5 min read Topics are working hypotheses, not keyword data

Two costs arrive after the purchase and never appear in a comparison table: the washing-up and the footprint. Both are measurable before you buy, if you are willing to use a tape measure and count parts.

Count the parts that touch food

Go through the listing and count every component that will need washing after a typical cook.

  • A single-drawer fryer: one drawer, one crisper plate. Two items.
  • A dual-basket fryer: two drawers, two plates. Four items — twice the sink load for the same meal.
  • A French-door oven: door glass, rack, drip tray, plus the accessories in the box. The Lagasse listing states six cooking accessories are included.

"Dishwasher safe" reduces the labour but does not remove the loading, unloading, and hand-washing of anything too big for the rack.

Measure the footprint properly

Three measurements, in this order:

  1. Counter depth. From the wall to the front edge. A large drawer needs clearance to pull out plus the drawer's own travel.
  2. Under-cabinet height. An oven-style unit with a top-hinged element needs lid clearance. French doors swing out, not up, which changes where the clearance has to be.
  3. Door or drawer swing. Tape the arc on the counter with painter's tape and live with it for a day. This sounds excessive until the first time you cannot open the drawer because the kettle is in the way.

The trade-off in one sentence

Every additional compartment buys you a capability and costs you a part to wash and a centimetre of counter. Decide which of those two you have more of.

If you are short on counter but long on cupboard

Store the unit rather than leave it out — but check the weight and whether the cord retracts, because a heavy oven-style unit that lives in a cupboard will stop being used by week three. That is an inference from how appliances get used, not a measured finding, and it is labelled as such.

Trade-off analysis on this page is editorial inference drawn from listing specifications, not from testing (S05). The parts counts come from the listing text observed 2026-11-20.
