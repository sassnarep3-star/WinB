How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The two costs that show up every single day, and the two that show up once at the till.

By Counterwise editorial Updated 2026-11-20 Reading time 6 min Basis published specifications and public guidance

In this guide

  2. Where the grease actually goes

Count the pieces that come out

One basket and one crisper plate is easy. A basket, a plate, a rack, a drip tray and a rotisserie spit is a small dishwashing project every time. Before buying, count the parts that touch food and decide how many you will genuinely wash by hand.

Dishwasher-safe is a claim about surviving the dishwasher, not about staying non-stick. Coatings dull with heat and detergent. If the coating matters to you, hand-wash the basket and let the accessories take the machine.

Where the grease actually goes

On a basket model, grease collects under the crisper plate and around the drawer slide. On an oven-style model, it collects on the door glass, the interior walls, and the element guard. The oven has more surface area and more places to reach with a cloth.

A removable, dishwasher-safe crumb tray is the single most useful cleaning feature on an oven-style machine. On a basket machine it is the plate, and almost all of them have one.

The counter-space maths

Three numbers, not one. Footprint: what it occupies permanently. Operating clearance: the extra room the drawer or door needs. Storage: whether it lives on the counter or comes out of a cupboard, because a machine that is annoying to retrieve stops being used.

Measure the height under your wall cabinets too. Basket models vent upward and many need several inches of clearance above the body.

Heat, steam, and what sits nearby

These machines exhaust hot, humid air. Where that vent points matters more than the machine's wattage: toward a wall, under a cabinet, or across a doorway all change how pleasant the thing is to own.

Keep the vent side clear and give it room. This is also the reason a machine that fits perfectly into a tight alcove can still be the wrong purchase.

A five-minute test at the shop or in the listing

Ask four questions. How many food-contact parts are there. Which of them are dishwasher-safe. Where is the vent, and which way does it point. And what is the exterior depth plus the drawer or door clearance, added together.

If the listing cannot answer all four, the machine has an information problem as well as a design one.

The short version

Cleanup and clearance are the costs you pay every week. Weigh them before the ones you pay once.
