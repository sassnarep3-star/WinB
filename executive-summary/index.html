Executive summary

What was built, what it can and cannot claim, what it needs from you, and the decisions that carry the most weight.

Build 2026-11-20 · associate tag shopnow047c-20 · 5 products · 8 guides · 3 worksheets · 20 flagged items · 14 proposals in the queue

In one paragraph

A five-product kitchen shop — three air fryers, two thermometers — built as a static publication with every Amazon link tagged and marked sponsored, the required Associates disclosure under the masthead on every page, no prices or ratings shown for policy reasons, and every unverifiable value left blank and flagged. The site reads as a shop: a magazine front page that opens with the five picks, two category hubs, a comparison table, five question-shaped guides, and a set of reviewable rooms for the decisions behind it all.

Three things were fixed this cycle that mattered more than any addition. First, the comparison and category tables were still printing “wattage — not stated” while the product pages published the same figures from the same listings; the tables now read from the one listing-evidence record, so a table cannot drift away from the page it summarises. Second, every table row now carries a source link, and the build refuses to publish if one does not — 208 data rows, 204 of them linking straight to the official source. Third, the publication ran under two names; it now runs under one, and nine superseded pages that were still indexable have been retired so that only one site competes for the same searches.

What has not changed is the honest part. No product has been handled, no traffic has been measured, and the supplied short links are still unconfirmed against the account. Those are the three decisions below, and everything else on this site is polish on top of them.

November 2026 refresh (S15)

This cycle added decision tools rather than more product copy: a 30-second chooser on the front page (“Which one fits your kitchen?”), three printable worksheets (capacity, cleanup/counter-space, thermometer calibration), three new question-shaped guides (holiday roast thermometer, game-day wings, air-fryer leftovers), and a buyer-questions FAQ matrix on the front page and both category hubs. All new guides are timely framing of existing, sourced material — editorial hypotheses for November, not popularity or demand claims. The chooser and worksheets run entirely in the browser: no account, no cookie, nothing sent anywhere. The build now enforces R15 (question-shaped new guides, no invented demand, chooser present, worksheets printable) in addition to R1–R14.

What this site can claim

  • Every product page is sourced to the manufacturer’s listing, with the ASIN printed and the read date recorded.
  • Every Amazon link is tagged and marked rel="sponsored".
  • The required disclosure appears directly under the masthead on every page, and again in the footer.
  • No price, rating or sales figure is reproduced anywhere — checked by the build, not by eye.
  • The USDA safe-temperature table is quoted from the agency’s own chart, with rest times.
  • Every stated wattage, temperature range, model number and warranty term is transcribed from the listing, with the phrase it came from recorded in _build/listing-evidence.mjs.
  • Review-theme counts are Amazon’s own published figures, attributed and dated, sorted worst-first.
  • The sitemap and RSS feed are generated by the build, so a new guide cannot ship without being listed in both.
  • Every row of every table carries a source link, checked by the build. 204 of the 208 data rows link straight to the official source; the rest point at the register entry that holds the evidence.
  • One name, one site. The publication runs under Counterwise everywhere, and the superseded generations are excluded from indexing so that only one site competes on this host.

What it cannot claim, and will not

  • No hands-on testing. None of the five products has been handled. Every page says so.
  • No traffic, clicks, orders or revenue. No analytics endpoint is deployed, so nothing has been measured. The campaign ledger is empty by design.
  • No confirmation that the supplied short links credit your account. The resolved URLs carry no tag; the site uses tagged canonical links instead.
  • No ranking or price comparison. Five products in two categories is a shop, not a market.
  • No claim that an unpublished number was published, or the reverse. Where a listing is silent, the cell is blank. Where it contradicts itself, both figures appear.

How it reads as a shop

  • The front page leads with the five picks, then the two category hubs, then the guides. Registers live in the footer and one “More” group, never in the hero.
  • Two clusters (air fryers, thermometers) link to each other and to the comparison table. A seasonal notebook reframes the guides by cooking moment as hypotheses — no popularity or search-volume claim is made.
  • Every guide and product page carries a byline (author, date, reading time). Imagery is generated editorial illustration, labelled as such; no Amazon photography is stored.
  • The automation pipeline (generation, sitemap, feed, R1–R15 gate, link crawl, smoke test) is tabled in the Data Room. Product facts, prose, topic choice and ledger rows stay human-gated.
  • The Design Room holds the design directions, the copy-ready prompt that feeds them, the organic-traffic playbook and the questions that have to be answered before a design decision is reasonable.

The honest assessment

The site is in good shape as a publication and in an early state as a business. It has a coherent product set, a defensible editorial position, one name, and no compliance debt that it has hidden. What it does not have is a single reader, a single confirmed click, or a product anyone here has touched. The three decisions above are the ones that change that; everything else is polish on top of them.

One thing to know about the shape of this site: the pages a buyer reads and the pages you read to check it are deliberately different. The shop is five picks, two categories, five guides and a comparison table. Everything else — the suggestions room, the decision room, the design room, the data room, the flagged list — sits behind the footer, because a reader who arrives from a search wants an answer, not a process.
