Executive summary

What was built, what it can and cannot claim, what it needs from you, and the decisions that carry the most weight.

Build 2026-11-20 · associate tag shopnow047c-20 · 5 products · 8 guides · 3 worksheets · 15 flagged items · 10 proposals in the queue

In one paragraph

A five-product kitchen shop — three air fryers, two thermometers — built as a static publication with every Amazon link tagged and marked sponsored, the required Associates disclosure above the masthead on every page, no prices or ratings shown for policy reasons, and every unverifiable value left blank and flagged. This cycle moved the site from a specification summary to a buying decision: the front page now opens with five buyer situations rather than a methodology statement, the comparison table carries stated wattage, temperature range and the weakest review theme per product, and each product page reports what repeat buyers complain about using the counts Amazon itself publishes.

Two things were also fixed that mattered more than any addition. The site had been reporting published specifications as “not stated” — wattage, temperature range and model numbers were all on the listings (FLG-014). And three listings contradict themselves on their own figures; both values are now printed rather than one being quietly chosen (FLG-013).

November 2026 refresh (S15)

This cycle added decision tools rather than more product copy: a 30-second chooser on the front page (“Which one fits your kitchen?”), three printable worksheets (capacity, cleanup/counter-space, thermometer calibration), three new question-shaped guides (holiday roast thermometer, game-day wings, air-fryer leftovers), and a buyer-questions FAQ matrix on the front page and both category hubs. All new guides are timely framing of existing, sourced material — editorial hypotheses for November, not popularity or demand claims. The chooser and worksheets run entirely in the browser: no account, no cookie, nothing sent anywhere. The build now enforces R15 (question-shaped new guides, no invented demand, chooser present, worksheets printable) in addition to R1–R14.

What this site can claim

  • Every product page is sourced to the manufacturer’s listing, with the ASIN printed and the read date recorded.
  • Every Amazon link is tagged and marked rel="sponsored".
  • The required disclosure appears above the masthead on every page, and again in the footer.
  • No price, rating or sales figure is reproduced anywhere — checked by the build, not by eye.
  • The USDA safe-temperature table is quoted from the agency’s own chart, with rest times.
  • Every stated wattage, temperature range, model number and warranty term is transcribed from the listing, with the phrase it came from recorded in _build/listing-evidence.mjs.
  • Review-theme counts are Amazon’s own published figures, attributed and dated, sorted worst-first.
  • The sitemap and RSS feed are generated by the build, so a new guide cannot ship without being listed in both.

What it cannot claim, and will not

  • No hands-on testing. None of the five products has been handled. Every page says so.
  • No traffic, clicks, orders or revenue. No analytics endpoint is deployed, so nothing has been measured. The campaign ledger is empty by design.
  • No confirmation that the supplied short links credit your account. The resolved URLs carry no tag; the site uses tagged canonical links instead.
  • No ranking or price comparison. Five products in two categories is a shop, not a market.
  • No claim that an unpublished number was published, or the reverse. Where a listing is silent, the cell is blank. Where it contradicts itself, both figures appear.

How it reads as a shop

  • The front page leads with the five picks, then the two category hubs, then the guides. Registers live in the footer and one “More” group, never in the hero.
  • Two clusters (air fryers, thermometers) link to each other and to the comparison table. A seasonal notebook reframes the guides by cooking moment as hypotheses — no popularity or search-volume claim is made.
  • Every guide and product page carries a byline (author, date, reading time). Imagery is generated editorial illustration, labelled as such; no Amazon photography is stored.
  • The automation pipeline (generation, sitemap, feed, R1–R10 gate, link crawl, smoke test) is tabled in the Data Room. Product facts, prose, topic choice and ledger rows stay human-gated.

What you can review, in order

  1. Suggestions and the decision queue — 10 proposals with cost, reversibility and failure mode, the 9 questions that block them, and the five actions only you can take (about fifty minutes total). Start here.
  2. Decision Room — three paths to choose between, a seasonal topic queue with measurement gates, 14 suggestions ordered by priority, 14 questions split into known and unknown, a missing-information discovery plan, and the options rejected with sources.
  3. Flagged for Review — 15 items that could not be confirmed, each with evidence and the shortest route to closing it.
  4. Data Room — the product-intent record, the source register, the automation pipeline, the organic playbook, the five events, and the empty ledger with its schema.

The honest assessment

The site is in good shape as a publication and in an early state as a business. It has a coherent product set, a defensible editorial position and no compliance debt that it has hidden. What it does not have is a single reader, a single confirmed click, or a product anyone here has touched. The three suggestions at priority 1 are the ones that change that; everything else is polish on top of them.
