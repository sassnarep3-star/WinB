Standards

How this site works

Five rules, applied to every page. An earlier version broke one of them by calling published specifications unpublished; that is recorded on the Flagged page and fixed. If a page breaks a rule, it is a bug.

Rule one: no hands-on claim without hands on the product

This desk has not used any of the five products. So no page says “we tested”, “in our kitchen”, “after a month of use”, or anything else that implies contact. Every page describes what the manufacturer’s listing states, shows the arithmetic, and lists what is missing.

The FTC is direct about this: you cannot talk about your experience with a product you have not tried. That is not a style preference we are being precious about — it is the reason the pages read the way they do. The word “review” in this site’s URLs is used in the older sense of an assessment; where it could mislead, the page says so in the first paragraph.

Rule two: no price, no rating, no rank

Amazon’s Program Policies allow a site to show prices only if Amazon serves the link containing that data, or if the data comes from the Creators API or Product Advertising API under the licence. This site is a set of static files with no API credentials, so it shows no prices. It also shows no customer star ratings and no popularity position, for the same reason and because those are Amazon’s content rather than this site’s judgement.

What that means in practice: every one of the five listings displayed a price and a customer rating when this desk read it, and this site reproduces neither. Not because they were unknown — they were right there — but because printing them here would be both stale within the hour and outside the rule. The comparison table has an empty price column on purpose, and the note under it explains why rather than quietly omitting it.

Rule three: every outbound Amazon link is a tagged, marked affiliate link

Each Amazon link is built from the observed ASIN plus the Associate tag shopnow047c-20, and carries rel="sponsored nofollow noopener". The tag is what makes a click attributable to this account; the sponsored value tells search engines the link is a paid placement, which is what Google asks for. Links are rel-marked in the HTML, so the marking survives with JavaScript disabled.

Each product page also offers a plainly-labelled untagged listing link, marked “no commission”, so a reader who does not want to use an affiliate link can still check the product. It is marked the same way for search engines.

Rule four: what cannot be sourced is blank, and labelled

Counter footprint is blank for all three air fryers. External dimensions for the two thermometers are blank. Probe length is blank. In each case the listing did not state it in the data this desk could read, and guessing a plausible range is the exact failure mode this project was set up to avoid. A blank with “not stated” beside it is a fact; a plausible number would be a fabrication.

The reverse rule applies too, and an earlier version of this page broke it. Wattage, temperature range, model numbers and warranty terms were published on the listings, and the site was reporting them as “not stated”. That was fixed on 2026-11-20: those values are now printed, transcribed from the listing, with the phrase they came from. See FLG-014. A specification only counts as missing when the listing is actually silent.

Where a single listing states two different values for the same thing, both are printed and the disagreement is flagged rather than quietly resolved — see FLG-013.

Every genuine blank is collected on the Flagged for Review page with the action that would resolve it.

Rule five: report what buyers actually complain about

Amazon publishes, above the reviews, a count of how many reviewers mentioned a given feature and how those mentions split positive and negative. It is the most decision-relevant thing on a product page and most buying guides ignore it. Each product page here reproduces those counts, attributed to Amazon, sorted worst-first, with a source line. They are not this desk’s sample and they are not a rating.

The disclosure, and why it is where it is

“As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” appears in a strip under the masthead on every page and again in the footer. It sits above the content rather than at the end because FTC guidance says disclosures are likely to be missed when they appear only on an about page or at the end of a post, and that they should be placed where they are hard to miss. The exact wording is the sentence named in section 5 of the Associates Program Operating Agreement.

What this site is not

  • It is not endorsed, sponsored or reviewed by Amazon. Amazon and the Amazon logo are trademarks of Amazon.com, Inc. or its affiliates.
  • It is not a consumer testing organisation. It compares published specifications.
  • It is not impartial in the sense of having no stake. It earns commission if you buy through its links, and says so at the top of every page.

Authorship, brand and imagery

Every guide and product page carries a byline — author, date and reading time — and the About page states who writes the site and what the desk will not do. Google’s site-reputation policy weighs stated authorship, and an unauthored affiliate page is its own example of content likely to attract action. The brand is Counterwise: kitchen gear, chosen by question rather than by hype. Imagery is generated editorial illustration, labelled as such. No Amazon product photography is stored, because the IP License forbids storing or caching Product Advertising Content images and a static site is a cache. Licensed brand photography is an open question (Q-14).

Seasons and clusters

The site is organised as two clusters — air fryers and thermometers — that link to each other and to the comparison table. A seasonal notebook reframes the existing guides by the meal being cooked (weeknight two-food dinners, big-batch sharing, holiday roasts, summer grilling) as editorial hypotheses, not as popularity claims. New seasonal guides are published one at a time, only after measurement says which one to write. See the topic queue.

Automation and the human gate

The build automates page generation, the sitemap, the feed, link checks and the R1–R10 compliance gate, and fails the publish on any breach. Product facts, guide prose, topic choice and ledger rows are human-gated: no AI step writes a product fact, and no ledger row is added without an authorised first-party report. The full pipeline is tabled in the Data Room.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, the fastest route is an issue on the repository behind this site. Corrections are logged, and a correction that changes a claim is dated on the page it changes.
