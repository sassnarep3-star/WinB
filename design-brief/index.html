Design brief · topic lab · decision input

What this site should look like, and what to write next

Everything on this page is a suggestion to review, not a change already made. The design directions below are ready to feed into a design prompt. The topic hypotheses are candidate guides, each with the measurement that must exist before it is promoted. The questions are the ones that must be answered before any decision can reasonably be made.

Read this with the implementation queue and the Data Room. Nothing here is implemented without an owner choice. No topic below is a claim about popularity, demand, search volume or product superiority.

How to use this page

  1. Choose a design path from the three options at the bottom of this page, or pick individual directions.
  2. Read the rationale and failure mode for each direction before adopting it — each is tied to a source.
  3. Treat every topic as a hypothesis. Publish a topic only after its measurement gate exists and a reader signal says it is useful.
  4. Answer the open questions that block the decision you want to make. Owner-only actions are marked as such.
  5. Do not implement from a suggestion without choosing it here. The build gate still runs on every change.

D1

Named authorship on every surface

A named human desk with a two-line biography on the front page, guides and product pages, not “the team”.

What changes

Add a consistent byline block (author, role, one or two lines of provenance) to the front page, every guide, worksheet and product page. Keep the existing “this desk has not handled any of these products” note next to it.

Why it helps a reader

Google’s site-reputation guidance weighs stated authorship, and an unauthored affiliate article is its own example of the problem. FTC guidance requires the material connection to be hard to miss; an accountable desk makes the disclosure credible.

Effort · reversible

Low · Yes

Failure mode to watch

None measured. The byline must not imply hands-on testing (FTC: no experience claims for untried products).

Prompt fragment — ready to paste into a design brief 
Design a byline block: a named author, a one-line role, and a one-line provenance note. Tone is a small editorial desk, not a brand department. It never implies hands-on testing.

Source: G2 · F1
