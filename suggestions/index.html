Suggestions for the next pass

A reviewable section, not a to-do list. Each suggestion states what it buys you, what it costs you, and the alternative paths, so a decision can be made by choosing between them rather than accepting or rejecting one option in the abstract.

How to read the scores

Impact and confidence are editorial judgements on a five-point scale, not measurements. No traffic, revenue, or conversion data exists yet, so any number here that looked precise would be invented. The open questions page lists what would have to be measured before these scores could become evidence.

Publish the question-shaped guides before the buying guides

SUG-01
Effort: Medium Impact 5/5 Confidence 4/5

Ship the four buyer-question articles first and let them carry internal links into the product comparisons. They answer something a person is actually stuck on, which is what earns a bookmark and a link.

The trade-offSlower path to a 'best of' page, which is the format most likely to convert first. Accept that in exchange for pages that can rank without competing head-on with established review sites.

Reader value

Show the trade-off, not a ranked winner

SUG-02
Effort: Low Impact 4/5 Confidence 4/5

Every product card leads with the one decision it settles, then the two or three specs that decide it. 'Two baskets or one big one' is more useful than a first, second, third ordering nobody asked for.

The trade-offNo single 'winner' headline, which is a weaker click from social. Stronger with a reader who is two searches deep and about to buy.

Trust

Keep the honesty flags visible instead of hidden in a repo

SUG-03
Effort: Low Impact 5/5 Confidence 5/5

The page that says 'we have not tested these' is a feature. Publish it in the footer navigation where a sceptical reader finds it in one click, and keep the unverified list on the product pages themselves.

The trade-offIt reads as less authoritative than a confident review with a score out of ten. It is also the only version that survives a compliance read.

Growth

Own one narrow question per article, in the reader's words

SUG-04
Effort: Medium Impact 5/5 Confidence 3/5

Titles should be a sentence a person would type. 'Basket or oven-style air fryer' is that shape. 'The Ultimate Air Fryer Buying Guide 2026' is not, and will lose to sites with more history.

The trade-offNarrow titles cap the traffic ceiling per page. They also convert far better than a broad page that answers nothing precisely.
