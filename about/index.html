The Crisp Kitchen Editorial Desk

Product research and verification

A research desk that reads manufacturer documentation and retailer listings line by line, keeps a dated record of every check, and publishes what could not be verified instead of guessing.

The byline is a desk, not a person, because no individual has agreed to be named as the author. That is stated rather than hidden behind a stock portrait: inventing a human reviewer would be the most obvious lie a site like this could tell.

What this site will do

  • Tell you which of five products suits which kind of cooking, and which one does not.
  • Label every sentence as a seller claim, our inference, or something we read off the listing ourselves.
  • Publish the things it could not verify, on a page you can reach from every footer.
  • Pay for itself through affiliate commission, disclosed on every page.

What this site will not do

  • Publish a price it cannot serve legally or keep current.
  • Publish a star rating copied from a page whose text mixes products together.
  • Claim a product was tried, tested or handled by this desk when it was not.
  • Estimate traffic, conversion rate or revenue for you.
  • Use a vague disclosure like "sp" or hide it at the bottom of the page.

How the site is built

The pages are generated from JSON data files by a small Node build script. That is not engineering vanity: it is what makes the compliance rules enforceable. The build fails if a product link is not marked sponsored, if a claim cites a source that is not registered, or if a page loses its disclosure. See the compliance gate.

There is no database, no login, no cookie and no third-party script. The only JavaScript on the site counts five events locally and, if an endpoint is configured, posts one small body to it.
