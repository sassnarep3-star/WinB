“8 Qt” sounds bigger than “6 Quart”, but a basket count and a total volume are not the same measurement. This checklist helps you read capacity claims before you believe them.

The checklist

  1. Is the number total or per basket? The CHEFMAN 6 Quart listing names a 6 Quart unit with “2 Independent 3Qt” baskets. Per-basket and total figures sit side by side there, so read both.
  2. What does a “serving” mean? Manufacturers do not define serving size the same way. Count what you actually cook: how many chicken thighs or fries fit in one layer.
  3. Check the product details table, not only the title. Titles are short. Dimensions and capacity details are usually in the listing’s specification area.
  4. Test your own batch. Put your usual portion on a sheet of paper or foil, measure it, and compare that to the basket.
  5. Read capacity-related reviews with care. Reviews are opinions, not measurements. Look for reviewers who describe their batch size.

Capacity figures from the listings

  • P-02 · Emeril Lagasse — “26QT” in the title, described as “Extra Large Interior” — Amazon listing
