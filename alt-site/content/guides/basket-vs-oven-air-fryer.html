Most people start by comparing brands. It works better to decide which shape of appliance fits your kitchen first. In this guide, “basket” means a drawer-style air fryer. “Oven-style” means a countertop unit with a door and racks.

Questions to answer before you compare

  • How many foods do you cook at the same time? Two foods at different temperatures is a different problem from one batch of fries.
  • How much counter height and depth do you have? Oven-style units usually need room for the door to open.
  • Do you want one function (air frying) or several (roast, bake, toast, dehydrate)?
  • Will you clean it by hand or in a dishwasher? Check the care instructions, not only the listing title.

A simple way to decide

If you cook for one or two people and want a small footprint, a basket style is usually the simpler choice. If you want to roast a tray, bake, and toast in one unit, an oven-style model is built for that job. Either way, measure your counter and the path the door or drawer needs before you open a listing.

Editorial note: This guide is based on listing text and general criteria. It makes no claim that one design cooks better than the other.
