Guide · Air fryers

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity.

By the Counter & Crisp Desk Published 2026-11-20 Cluster: Air fryers ~6 min read All guides

Capacity numbers on boxes are like screen inches on TVs: technically true, practically slippery. Two "6 qt" machines can hold very different dinners. Here is how to compare what fits, without trusting a number we can't see measured.

The short version

Judge capacity by what fits in one layer, not by quarts in a headline. Get the interior dimensions from the listing (ask the seller if absent), then map them to the foods you actually cook. Households don't need "more capacity" in the abstract — they need one less batch on the nights that matter.

Why quart numbers mislead

Capacity figures are manufacturer statements. We have not verified how each brand measures them, and shapes confound comparison: a tall 8 qt drawer and a wide 6 qt chamber hold different shapes of food. A number you can't see measured is a number you can only use for rough sorting — so let's do the rough sorting honestly, then move to what fits.

The checklist

  • Get the interior, not the exterior. Ask: "What are the usable interior dimensions with the rack/basket in cooking position?" Sellers answer this; boxes rarely say.
  • Think in layers. Air frying depends on surface contact. Capacity that requires heaping means steaming, not crisping. Plan for one layer plus a shake.
  • Name your biggest night. Not the average Tuesday — the night four people want fries and something else needs the oven. Can one batch cover it?
  • Count the batches, not the quarts. A useful thought experiment: for your two most-cooked foods, how many loads would each candidate need? One load beats two almost every time.
  • For oven styles, count racks — and rack spacing. Two racks that fit simultaneously mean something only if both can hold your actual tray.
  • Whole birds and roasts: measure the food you buy, not the appliance's adjectives. A 4 lb chicken needs interior length and height you can verify.
  • Check the recall list. Before you buy any model, search it in CPSC's recall database.

Capacity by household — as questions, not rules

Nobody on this desk has weighed your household, so here is the decision as questions:

Capacity decision aid. No serving-size numbers are published here because we cannot source them; your meals are the data.
If your reality is…Ask yourself…
Cooking for one or two most nightsWould a compact basket that preheats in minutes beat a big chamber you half-fill?
Family sides every nightDoes one batch of the most-cooked side feed everyone, or will you run it twice — every night?
Weekend hostingIs the big night served by one wide load (oven style) or two synced loads (dual basket)?
Holiday cookingDoes the appliance help with the main event, or is it relief capacity for the sides while the oven does the bird?

One test to run at home (after purchase)

Buy your most-cooked food once in its usual package and load it in a single layer the first time you use the machine. Whatever fits there is your real capacity. Write it on a sticky note inside the cabinet door. That number — yours — beats anything on the box.
